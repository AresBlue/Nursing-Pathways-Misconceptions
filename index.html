Different Nursing Careers, Challenging Misconceptions about Nursing

Nursing is a diverse, skilled profession, that plays a major role in the healthcare, seen as the "backbone". While caring for patients is the heart of the career, the role involves much more than people realise. Nurses use medical knowledge, clinical skills, critical thinking, and judgement. They monitor their conditions, they administer medications, recognise changes in health, and most importantly advocate for patient.

Nurses also work collaboratively with doctors and their other healthcare professionals, while maintaining their own professional responsibilities. Their work requires communication, teamwork, and emotional resilience, particularly when supporting patients and families through difficult experiences.

Nursing is not just a single job, it offers multiple different work environments and specialities, allowing nurses to develop their skills and care throughout their careers.

Reality of Nursing

"If the doctor says something that we don't agree with, we can deny it; you need to be a patient advocate"

- Nurse Unit Manager, Intensive Care Unit

This highlights the importance of nurses having professional judgement and advocating for their patients. Nurses work alongside doctors and other healthcare professionals, but they also have responsibilities to question concerns, communicate their clinical judgement and help ensure patient safety.

My research showed a clear gap of knowledge between the public perceptions of nursing and the experiences described from nurses themselves. By exploring the core responsibilities of nursing, challenging misconceptions, and highlighting pathways and specialties, this website aims to improve community understanding of the profession.

Nursing is more then a job, or stereotype, it is a profession built on university grade knowledge, contains high responsibilities and advocacy.

Summary of Misconceptions

Some members of the public misunderstand the profession. Common misconceptions included the beliefs that nursing is an easy job, that nurses simply "work for and follow doctors" My community survey found that 56.3% of participants believed nurses work for doctors and follow their commands. However, my research showed their roles involve much more independent decision-making, clinical responsibilities, teamwork, and patient advocacy.
