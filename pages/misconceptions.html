Misconceptions of Nursing

Nursing if often associated with caring for people, hosptials and helping/working for doctors. Although caring is a big part in nursing, these sterotypes do not represent the knowedlge, responsbilities, clinical skills, and emotional demands involved career.

Through my research, I discovered that misconceptions about nursing can affect how peple understand the career. To investigate this, I conducted surveys and interviews and compared them with infromation.

What my Research revealed?

56.3% belived nurses worked for doctors and follow their commands

My school community survey showed that more than half of the participants held this belief. This was significant because my research with nurses showed that, although nurses collaborate closely with doctors, they do have their own responsbilites, clinical jugement, and scope of practice.

Misconception 1: Nurses simply follow doctors' orders.

Nurses and doctors work together to provide a safe patient care, but they do have different responsbilites, nurses access patients, monitor changes in their condition, admister medications within their scope of practice, and advocate for paitents.

During one interview, a nurse unit manager working in intensive care explained:

"If the doctor says something that we don't agree with, we can deny it; you need to be a patient advocate."

Misconeption 2: Nursing is just caring for people.

Yes nurses do care for people, but nursing involves much more. Depending on their role and scope of practice, nurses amy access patients, interpret infomration, administer medications, recgonise changes in health, and assit with MANY procedures, and contribute to treatment.

My research showed that nurses need medical knowledge, and practical skills, critical thinking to provide safe care. Caring for someone need understanding of their physical, emotional needs, and recognise risks and responding appropriately.

Misconception 3: Nursing is an easy job.

Nursing is rewarding, but also can be physically, mentally, and emotionally demanding. Nurses may work long shifts, weekends, and holidays, and mangage distressed families, responding to emergencies, and make important decision under pressure, this also has another layer of abuse ontop of this...

My Nurse Unit Manger Interview highlighted the emotional demands of nursing. They explained that people many not understand how difficult it cna be to sit beside someone who dying, while ensuring their dignity maintained.

This helped me understand the emotional side of nursing, can be significnat as clincal responsibilites.

Misconeption 4: Nursing requires not much medical knowedgle.

Nursing education requires learning clinical expeirence, knoweldge of anatomy, medication, health conditions, infection, patient assessment and many more. The excat responsbilites vary depedning on experince, workplace, and scope of practice. My research found that nursing requries ongoing learning and development rather then just basic tasks.

Misconception 5: Nursing is only one job.

One of the most significant findings from my research was how nursing pathways are unknown to the public. Nurses can work in...Emergency, icu, travel, and many many more, more detalied list is provided in Nursing Specialties Area. :)
