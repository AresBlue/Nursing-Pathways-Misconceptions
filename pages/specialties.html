Specialties in Nursing

One profession, many more different roles.

Nursing is a diverse profession that extends well beyond general hospital ward. Nurses care for people in different stages of life, supporitng patients with different conditions, and work in range of environments. Some nurses focus on particular patient group or medical area. Others work in community services, education, leadership, and specialsied enviroments. My research identifed several specialites that were not weidely recognised by members of the community. The nurses I surveyed also identifed areas such as mental health, critical care, aged care, nurse practioners, and nurse consultants as roles people may not understand. The following examples of roles demonstrate the differences between roles yet sharing the same foundations.

Hosptial Specialities

Emergency Nursing

Emergency nurses care for people who arrive at emergency departments, working as teams with other healthcare profefesionals dealing with urgent health concerns. The enviroment is fast-paced, requiring teamwork, clinical reaoing, and ability to respond when the situation changes.

Criticla Care

Critical Care Nurses care for patients with serious or life-threatening conditions who require close monitoring and complex treatment.This area requries cinical knowledge, careful assessment, attention to detail and effective teamwork. My interview with a Nurse Unit Manager in an ICU helped me understand the responsibilities involved in both bedside care and maintaining a safe clinical environment.

Specialised and alternative nrusing envionments

Travel Nursing

Travel Nurses take temporary placements in different healthcare services. These placements may be in rural, regional or remote communities, as well as other locations where services need additional nursing staff.Working in teams, procedures and patient needs. Depending on the placement, they may work in hospitals, aged care or other healthcare services. This way can provide experience across different settings while helping services respond to workforce shortages. Requirements depend on the role and placement, and nurses need appropriate registration, experience and skills.

Nurse Practioner

Nurse Practioners are expeinced registered nurses who completed further education and obtained the required education. They have an expanded scope of practice. Theyt work in areas such as primary care, emergency srvieces, or any other specialised areas.

Nurse Unit Manager

A nurse unit manger oversees the operation of a nursing unit or department, their responsiblites include staffing, rostering, staff support, patient safety, resource maagement, and addressing concerns and help maintain a safe enviroment for patient care and staff. My interview with the ICU nurse unit manger showed leadership invovles much more than managing staff, it reuqires balancing patient safety, the needs of families, and staff wellbeing, and even sometimes helping out with patients when things get hard.
