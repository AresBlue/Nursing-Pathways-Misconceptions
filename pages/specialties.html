Specialties in Nursing

One profession, many more different roles.

Nursing is a diverse profession that extends well beyond general hospital ward. Nurses care for people in different stages of life, supporting patients with different conditions, and work in range of environments. Some nurses focus on particular patient group or medical area. Others work in community services, education, leadership, and specialised environments. My research identified several specialities that were not widely recognised by members of the community. The nurses I surveyed also identified areas such as mental health, critical care, aged care, nurse practitioners, and nurse consultants as roles people may not understand. The following examples of roles demonstrate the differences between roles yet sharing the same foundations.

Hospital Specialities

Emergency Nursing

Emergency nurses care for people who arrive at emergency departments, working as teams with other healthcare professionals dealing with urgent health concerns. The environment is fast-paced, requiring teamwork, clinical reasoning, and ability to respond when the situation changes.

Critical Care

Critical Care Nurses care for patients with serious or life-threatening conditions who require close monitoring and complex treatment. This area requires clinical knowledge, careful assessment, attention to detail and effective teamwork. My interview with a Nurse Unit Manager in an ICU helped me understand the responsibilities involved in both bedside care and maintaining a safe clinical environment.

Specialised and alternative nursing environments

Travel Nursing

Travel Nurses take temporary placements in different healthcare services. These placements may be in rural, regional or remote communities, as well as other locations where services need additional nursing staff. Working in teams, procedures and patient needs. Depending on the placement, they may work in hospitals, aged care or other healthcare services. This way can provide experience across different settings while helping services respond to workforce shortages. Requirements depend on the role and placement, and nurses need appropriate registration, experience and skills.

Nurse Practitioner

Nurse Practitioners are experienced registered nurses who completed further education and obtained the required education. They have an expanded scope of practice. They work in areas such as primary care, emergency services, or any other specialised areas.

Nurse Unit Manager

A nurse unit manger oversees the operation of a nursing unit or department, their responsibilities include staffing, rostering, staff support, patient safety, resource management, and addressing concerns and help maintain a safe environment for patient care and staff. My interview with the ICU nurse unit manger showed leadership involves much more than managing staff, it requires balancing patient safety, the needs of families, and staff wellbeing, and even sometimes helping out with patients when things get hard.
